## What the results mean

- Random forest was selected because it had the highest F1 score on the separate selection group (0.570 compared with Logistic regression at 0.243). The final check played no part in that choice.
- The final check held 2000 readings that the models never saw during training or selection. 68 of them were actual failures.
- Random forest found 52 of the 68 actual failures and missed 16.
- It raised 92 warnings and 40 were false alarms. About 6 of every 10 warnings were correct.
- A rule that always predicts no failure would be right on 96.6% of final-check readings but it would find none of the 68 actual failures. That is why this page reports missed failures instead of overall accuracy.
- Logistic regression found 4 more failures (56 compared with 52) but raised 286 more false alarms (326 compared with 40).
- Neither model is better on both counts. The right balance depends on the cost of a missed failure compared with a false alarm.
- These results come from generated equipment data split into random rows. They describe how the model sorted held-out readings. They do not forecast when a real machine will break down or prove performance on real equipment.
